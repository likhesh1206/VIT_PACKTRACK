🎓 Faculty Defense Dossier: Are Smart Bulbs & IoT Devices Really Common & Vulnerable?

Hard Academic & Market Evidence

Why this is crucial for Computer Networks (CN): Skeptics often assume smart bulbs or plugs are trivial toys. In reality, smart bulbs (Philips Hue, Wipro, Syska) and smart plugs are the #1 entry-level IoT devices sold in India and globally (₹400–₹800 on Amazon/Flipkart with over 250,000+ consumer reviews). Because they run full microkernels or RTOS (e.g. ESP8266/ESP32) on home Wi-Fi without antivirus, attackers do not hack bulbs to turn off lights—they hack them to weaponize them into DDoS botnet nodes and pivot across the home LAN into laptops and network storage.

📌 Paper #1 Citation: Antonakakis et al. (Mirai botnet infected 600K+ IoT devices) 📌 Paper #2 Citation: Sivanathan et al. (UNSW deployed real Philips Hue & LIFX bulbs) 📌 Famous CVE: CVE-2020-6007 (Check Point Research hacked Philips Hue to control home PCs)
💡

Smart WiFi RGB Bulb

Philips Hue • Wipro • Syska
CVE-2020-6007
Household Reality: Best-selling entry IoT device in India (₹449 on Amazon/Flipkart). Over 10M units deployed. Controlled via Google Home and Alexa.
Documented Attack: Check Point Research demonstrated a heap buffer overflow in Philips Hue bulbs allowing remote hackers to bridge onto the internal LAN and install ransomware on homeowner laptops.
Academic Ground Truth: Sivanathan et al. (UNSW) & IoT-23
🎙️

Amazon Echo Dot (Alexa)

Amazon Alexa • Google Nest
CVE-2020-10177
Household Reality: Over 500 million Amazon Echo & Alexa units sold worldwide. Present in millions of student bedrooms, living rooms, and labs.
Documented Attack: DNS rebinding and acoustic remote command injection allows remote adversaries to trigger unauthenticated local LAN API calls.
Academic Ground Truth: N-BaIoT (Meidan 2018) & UNSW
📹

WiFi Pan/Tilt CCTV Camera

TP-Link Tapo • Xiaomi Mi
Mirai Botnet Target
Household Reality: Top-selling Wi-Fi indoor CCTV in Indian apartments (₹1,999 on Amazon). Streams encrypted RTSP/TLS video to mobile apps.
Documented Attack: Mirai botnet infected over 600,000 consumer security cameras with default passwords to launch a 1.2 Tbps DDoS that crippled Dyn DNS.
Academic Ground Truth: Antonakakis et al. (Mirai Paper #1)
📺

Smart TV & FireStick 4K

Samsung • Amazon Fire TV
ADB.Miner Botnet
Household Reality: Present in 85%+ modern living rooms. Connected to Wi-Fi 24/7 running streaming video services (Netflix, Prime, YouTube).
Documented Attack: ADB.Miner worm scanned port 5555 to silently install Monero cryptocurrency miners and botnet agents that execute background DDoS floods.
Academic Ground Truth: IoT-23 Malware Captures

16A High-Power Smart Plug

Wipro 16A • TP-Link Tapo P110
CVE-2023-40152
Household Reality: Extremely common across Indian homes for scheduling air conditioners (ACs), water geysers, and RO purifiers.
Documented Attack: Researchers discovered unauthenticated local UDP control ports allowing malicious firmware injection to recruit smart plugs into botnets.
Academic Ground Truth: Meidan et al. (Belkin/TP-Link)

Smart Video Doorbell

Amazon Ring • Google Nest
CVE-2019-9483
Household Reality: Front-door video intercom with two-way audio and motion detection. Millions installed in urban apartments.
Documented Attack: Setup mode leaked homeowner Wi-Fi PSK passwords in cleartext, enabling neighbors/attackers to join the local network.
